# What confuses a dispatcher in the AI cards

Walk-through of the assistant panel (Decisions, Delayed at the gate, Heads-up, Done) on the Sep 4, 2025 board at 09:50.
Recorded in the dispatcher's own words, before any redesign.

## Overall (Oct 13, 2026)

> "AI suggestions right now, they very vague. In order to understand them, you need to really dive in into a topic. I want to feel super simple. For example: this family is late, let's combine this with that, so everyone is gonna be happy. And then when we pull the detail view, then we're gonna see why that decision was suggested, because the family would be waiting more than 25 minutes, etc., per our policy."

What this asks for:
- The card leads with a one-line story in plain words: what happened, and the move.
- The reasons (the 25-min wait limit, in-house first, never split a family) move into the detail view.

## Card 1 · Group 3 van broke

> "Keep super little text. What you're saying in paragraph two? It is correct, but that's a lot to read. Put that in details."

> "We moved AI right next to the grid so that AI can show what rides it is going to combine. Please make that animation."

- The facts paragraph (AS 920 39 min late, lands 11:35, three families ready at 11:00, 65 min wait, limit 25) is right, but it's too much to read on the card. It belongs in the details.
- The card itself should carry very little text.
- The panel sits beside the grid on purpose: the assistant should **show** on the grid which rides it will combine, as an animation, instead of describing it in words.
- Not raised (my guesses, not confirmed): "van broke" read as a breakdown, "Group 3" / "Sprinter 1" as internal numbers, "act by 10:40" with no reason, Cost line vs the $0 option.

Reaction to the proposed grid animation (Kelli Rae's family lifts out of the van row into its own SUV row with Kyle):

> "Card 1 good, super simple."

## Card 2 · No driver for Kevin Doyle

> "Title of the card is bad. Details are good. Brandi and Diane are combining while Kevin drops out, and then there are details. Since you already know the title, it says very little, and then when you go to the details, you see why and what."

- **The title names the wrong thing.** "No driver for Kevin Doyle" is the symptom, but the move is Brandi Sutton and Diane Prescott riding together. The title should say the move.
- The facts (Kevin ready 13:11, nobody assigned; two Lumora couples in two sedans; they land six minutes apart) are good, but they belong in the details.
- The story order: title = the move, then very little text, then the details say why and what.
- My proposed animation ("Brandi Sutton's row slides into Diane Prescott's row, and the two become one SUV. Then Mike's name drops into Kevin Doyle's empty driver cell.") was rated **bad**. It should read as one simple picture: Brandi and Diane combine, and Kevin's problem goes away.

## Stopped at card 3: the problem is the schedule, not the cards

> "Let's scratch everything. The problem started from the problem itself, because first we need to look at the schedule. The schedule that we have, of groups being separated, is not well executed. For the problem to solve, first we're gonna make rides that are late, and I'll combine them by myself, and then I will explain to you what I did, with the logic, and the logic will fit to the AI dispatch."

- The staged problems behind the cards don't hold up, so better wording can't fix the cards.
- New order: (1) set up a board of late rides, with nothing fixed yet; (2) the dispatcher combines them by hand; (3) the dispatcher explains each move and the logic behind it; (4) that logic becomes the AI's rules, and the cards come from it.
- Cards 3 and 4, the gate delays and the Heads-up section were not reviewed.
